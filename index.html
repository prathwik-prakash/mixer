A mixer earns its place on the counter the way a good knife earns its place in the hand — through years of quiet, unglamorous reliability. We built ours backwards from that idea.

Mechanism

Planetary motion, the old way

The beater spins on its own axis while orbiting the bowl — the same path bakers have relied on for a century. It reaches every inch of the bowl in every rotation, so nothing hides at the edges unmixed.

Body

Die-cast, not moulded

A single cast-metal body means the head, arm and base move as one — no flex, no rattle at high speed. It's heavier than it needs to be, on purpose. Weight is what keeps it planted on the counter at full tilt.

Control

Ten speeds, one dial

From a fold-in stir to a full-speed whip, the dial resists just enough that you feel each notch pass under your thumb. No screen, no menu — a control you can operate by feel alone, eyes on the bowl.
